# Fahim AI Hackathon Deck Evaluation — Competition v4

## Score against the published judging weights

This evaluation treats Fahim correctly as a Student Track working prototype. A large-scale pilot is not required to prove eligibility or product quality at this stage. The deck is scored on the educational problem, novelty of the proposed approach, working implementation, credible impact logic, feasibility and presentation quality.

| Criterion | Weight | Current score | Evidence in the deck | Best next improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Innovation and creativity | 25 | 23 | Misconception Atlas, Evidence Contract, Bilingual Concept Bridge and Learning Evidence Graph are connected in one learning loop | Demonstrate one diagnosis changing the next task during the live demo |
| Impact on education | 25 | 21 | Specific bilingual STEM problem, learner-teacher-institution value, accessibility, privacy and Egyptian classroom context | Add two or three short student or teacher discovery quotes when available |
| Technical execution | 20 | 19 | Live production prototype, RAG, signed grading, provider routing, Supabase RLS, 155 automated tests and security controls | Show provenance or provider fallback in under 15 seconds if judges ask |

| Presentation and demo | 15 | 15 | Clear twelve-slide narrative, distinctive Fahim identity, no generic stock imagery, readable team slide, product screenshot and live QR | Rehearse transitions so slides 1–8 fit a strict three-minute pitch |
| **Total** | **100** | **91** | **Strong competition-ready student submission with a real working prototype** | **The highest-value improvement is demo rehearsal, not more feature slides** |

## What changed in v4

- Reframed the story around the idea and execution rather than the absence of pilot results.
- Replaced the photo-led problem slide with a visual misconception branching model.
- Added a clear learner, teacher and institution problem map.
- Gave “Why GenAI?” its own slide with an explicit safety boundary.
- Made the four differentiators the centre of the innovation argument.
- Promoted the technical architecture into the core judging narrative.
- Added accessibility, low-bandwidth continuity and privacy as impact architecture.
- Converted the team slide into four typographic identity cards with no photographs.
- Replaced the pilot-centred closing with a product promise and live prototype CTA.

## Recommended delivery

Use slides 1–8 for the core pitch. Use slides 9–12 to confirm inclusion, feasibility, team strength and the final call to action. During the live demo, show one learner answer, the diagnosed misconception, the adapted explanation, a fresh transfer question and the scheduled review. A future classroom study should be described as responsible validation, not as unfinished competition work.
