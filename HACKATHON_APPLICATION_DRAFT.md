## Proposed product description

Fahim is an Arabic-first learning platform designed to make understanding inspectable rather than equating activity with mastery. A learner explains their reasoning, receives a reviewable misconception hypothesis and a focused intervention, retries a task, and returns for later recall. The interface connects recorded attempts, available source metadata, and progress evidence while distinguishing untested dimensions from recorded or explicitly verified evidence. Teachers are presented with a privacy-conscious learning workflow rather than unrestricted access to private conversations.

## Problem and differentiation

Correct answers can conceal incorrect reasoning. Generic chat and content consumption do not necessarily show whether a learner can explain, apply, or retain a concept independently. Fahim focuses its demonstration on an attempt-to-evidence loop, with source disclosure, misconception comparison, and review scheduling. Learning gains and diagnostic accuracy have not yet been established in a controlled pilot and must not be presented as measured results.

## Evidence that can be claimed

The current engineering check has 98 regression tests and 12 deterministic learning-control checks. These are engineering checks, not a pedagogical accuracy score, independent security certification, or measured student outcome. The project includes illustrative demo content labeled as such.
